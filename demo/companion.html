Law 4 Warriors

Veteran Companion

“A hand to hold, all the way to the decision.”

The follow-up assistant that walks beside the veteran between intake and decision — kind, patient, and persistent. It never nags and never guilt-trips. It just keeps the next small step in front of him, and celebrates every one he finishes.

What it is: the deadline engine knows every date in the case. The Companion is how those dates reach the veteran — as friendly texts and voice reminders that ask for one small step at a time, framed by how far he’s already come.

How it talks: every finished piece is celebrated (“Got it, James — that’s another piece in place”). Every ask is measured against his finish line (“Three pieces left and your file is complete”). And when life gets in the way, it re-opens the door with zero guilt — because a veteran who feels backed keeps going, and a veteran who feels chased disappears.

If he goes quiet — the ladder leans in, gently

1

A friendly text

One small step, two-tap reply. Warm, short, easy to answer from a lock screen.

2

An even simpler text

Shorter, plainer, zero guilt — “Yesterday got away from us. One small thing today?”

3

A voice call

For the veteran who doesn’t read texts, the assistant calls and says it out loud — like the samples above.

4

A person checks in

If he’s still quiet, a person from the team checks in personally — a human call, because some moments need one.

Nobody falls through silently. But nobody is ever made to feel like a problem, either — every rung is an offer of help, not an alarm about him.

The guard rails

Built in, not bolted on

  • Consent first. The Companion only starts with the veteran’s permission, captured at intake — and it stops the moment he says stop.
  • Quiet hours. Nothing arrives before 9 in the morning or after 8 at night, in his time zone.
  • Frequency caps. Never more than one message a day — and it pauses as soon as he replies. A conversation, not a drip campaign.
  • Humans on the hard moments. Any confused, upset, or distressed reply routes straight to a person on the team, immediately. The assistant never tries to talk a veteran through a crisis.
  • It knows its lane. The Companion never gives legal advice and never discusses ratings or outcomes — that’s the lawyer’s job. It handles steps, dates, and encouragement.
  • Progress, not failures. It speaks only about what’s been accomplished and what’s next — never about what he’s missed.
