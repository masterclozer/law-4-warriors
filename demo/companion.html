Law 4 Warriors

Veteran Companion — somebody kind, next to him the whole way

“A hand to hold, all the way to the decision.”

A texting and voice-call assistant driven by the deadline engine — a hand-holder, not a reminder service. It walks beside the veteran between intake and decision: kind, patient, and persistent. It never nags and never guilt-trips. It just keeps the next small step in front of him, and celebrates every one he finishes.

What it is: the deadline engine knows every date in the case. The Companion is how those dates reach the veteran — as friendly texts and voice reminders that ask for one small step at a time, framed by how far he’s already come.

How it talks: every finished piece is celebrated (“Got it, James — that’s another piece in place”). Every ask is measured against his finish line (“Three pieces left and your file is complete”). And when life gets in the way, it re-opens the door with zero guilt — because a veteran who feels backed keeps going, and a veteran who feels chased disappears.

If he doesn’t text

The same nudge, as a voice call

Some veterans never answer a text. So the Companion calls instead — a short, calm text-to-speech call that says the same one small thing out loud, and nothing more:

Voice call · spoken lines (TTS)
“Hi James, this is your team at Law 4 Warriors, just walking with you.”
“Welcome back. Here’s the one small thing that moves your case today: the first name of one buddy who served with you.”
“You can say the name now, or just press any key and we’ll text it to you instead. Either way is fine.”
“Thanks, James. We’ll take it from here.”

Thirty seconds, one ask, a kind exit. If he doesn’t pick up, the ladder below decides what happens next — never a second call the same day.

If he goes quiet — the ladder leans in, gently

1

A friendly text

One small step, two-tap reply. Warm, short, easy to answer from a lock screen.

2

An even simpler text

Shorter, plainer, zero guilt — “Yesterday got away from us. One small thing today?”

3

A voice call

For the veteran who doesn’t read texts, the assistant calls and says it out loud — like the samples above.

4

If he’s still quiet, a human being from the team calls — because some moments need one.

Nobody falls through silently. But nobody is ever made to feel like a problem, either — every rung is an offer of help, not an alarm about him.

The guard rails

The guard rails, in writing

  • Consent, captured at intake. The Companion only starts with the veteran’s permission — and it stops the moment he says stop.
  • His hours only. Messages and calls arrive only between 9am and 8pm, in his local time.
  • One nudge a day, at most — and it stops the moment he replies. A conversation, not a drip campaign.
  • Ground covered, never misses. Progress is counted as ground covered. Nothing he sees ever tallies what he didn’t do.
  • One word is banned. The word “overdue” never appears in anything he sees — deadlines are the firm’s problem, never his shame.
  • Humans on the hard moments. Anything distressed or confused goes straight to a human on the team, immediately. The assistant never tries to talk a veteran through a crisis.
  • It knows its lane. The Companion never gives legal advice and never discusses ratings or outcomes — that’s the lawyer’s job. It handles steps, dates, and encouragement.
