The Evidence Locker

A VA claim is won or lost on evidence. The locker collects it as we go — one small ask at a time — so the veteran is never handed a thirty-item homework list.

How it works: every condition in the claim needs the same three things — ① a current diagnosis, ② what happened in service, and ③ the connection (nexus) between them. The locker tracks all three for every condition in James R. Calloway’s file, and the system quietly goes and gets whatever is missing: records requests, buddy statements, doctor packets, and a symptom diary that builds itself.

This is the gap report from the decision analyzer, turned into a to-do list the system works — not a pile of chores dumped on the veteran.

The nexus packet — the doctor’s job, made easy

What the system assembles for the doctor

When a nexus slot stays empty, the system doesn’t just note it — it builds the packet that closes it, and tracks it like a deadline:

  • A one-page records summary — the relevant service entries and current findings, in date order, so the doctor isn’t handed 400 pages.
  • The exact question — is this condition connected to his service?
  • What a valid opinion must contain — the “more likely than not” standard, a medical rationale (not just a conclusion), and a statement that the records were reviewed.
  • A blank statement form with the veteran’s information already filled in.
✓ Packet assembled — Oct 14 ✓ Sent to his doctor — Oct 15 ⏳ Opinion expected — by Nov 5 Filed to the locker

For Calloway’s right knee: VA denied the connection because the service records are silent. A doctor’s reasoned opinion plus Mike’s statement is exactly how that denial gets answered on appeal.
