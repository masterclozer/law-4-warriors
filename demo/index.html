The Claims System — Live Demo

One veteran’s paperwork, followed end to end: a VA decision letter comes in, and claims, forms, and deadlines come out — with nothing typed twice.

What you’re looking at: a working walkthrough of the system that has already been built and verified as a proof of concept. Every page and document here uses the same fictional sample case — James R. Calloway — so you can follow one file through the whole pipeline. Nothing here is a mockup of the pipeline steps themselves: the letter, the record, the filled forms, and the deadline digest are the real outputs.

The pipeline, in five steps

1

A VA decision letter arrives

This is the VA’s paperwork — the rating decision the firm receives (or the veteran brings in). Ratings, dates, and deadlines are buried in letters like this one, and today someone in the office reads it and types everything out by hand.

Read the sample letter (PDF)

Fictional sample, watermarked on every page — the same letter the rest of this demo runs on.

2

The system reads it

The letter is extracted into the case’s master record — the single source of truth for the whole file. Identity, service, every rating with its diagnostic code and percentage, the decision date, and the appeal deadline the system computes from it.

19 of 19 extracted fields verified against the letter

See the master record
3

Forms fill themselves

That one record auto-populates the official VA forms — the real, current forms from VA.gov, downloaded for this proof. The lawyer reviews a completed, still-editable packet instead of typing one.

81 fields filled · 81 of 81 read back and verified exact

  • VA Form 20-0995 — Supplemental Claim
  • VA Form 20-0996 — Higher-Level Review
  • VA Form 10182 — Board Appeal
  • VA Form 21-22a — Appoint a representative
How this was verified — field by field
4

Deadlines stand guard

VA practice runs on unforgiving clocks — the one-year appeal window above all. The deadline engine starts the clock from the decision date on the letter itself and escalates alerts as dates approach. Every Monday, the firm gets one page: every case, every clock, worst first.

See the Monday Docket Digest

Sample digest with four fictional cases exercising every alert tier — overdue, critical, watch, on track.

5

She connects her own Adobe

The system ships with no vendor account embedded. The document-reading engine (Adobe Acrobat Services) is connected by the firm, under the firm’s own name — billing, audit trail, and the off-switch all belong to the firm. Credentials sit in an encrypted vault on the firm’s own server.
